Percolation Threshold

Each cell in the grid is independently "open" with probability p. Does a connected path of open cells span from the top row to the bottom row? Sweep p up from 0 and watch: for small grids nothing much happens until, quite suddenly, a giant connected cluster snaps into existence and spans the whole board. That jump is a genuine phase transition, and for a large square lattice it happens right around p ≈ 0.592746 — a constant nobody has ever found a closed-form expression for.

Finite-size scaling

Monte-Carlo estimate of "probability the grid percolates" as a function of p, for three grid sizes. Bigger grids make the S-curve snap sharper — in the infinite-size limit it becomes a true step function at pc.

Everything on this page is plain HTML5 canvas + vanilla JS, no dependencies. Spanning detection uses union-find with path compression: open cells are unioned with their open right/down neighbors, plus every open cell in the top row is unioned to a virtual TOP node and every open cell in the bottom row to a virtual BOTTOM node. The grid percolates the instant find(TOP) === find(BOTTOM).
